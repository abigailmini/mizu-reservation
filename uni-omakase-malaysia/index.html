What Makes Uni So Special?

Uni is the edible gonad of the sea urchin — but forget the biology, because what lands on your tongue is pure umami and cream. Great uni is sweet with a clean, oceanic finish and a texture somewhere between custard and butter. Poor uni is bitter, watery and metallic. The gap between the two is enormous, and it comes down to species, origin, freshness and handling.

That's exactly why uni rewards a dedicated tasting: side by side, you learn to taste the difference.

Sourcing: Why Hokkaido Matters

The world's most prized uni comes from the cold, nutrient-rich waters around Hokkaido, Japan. The cold slows the urchin's metabolism and concentrates sweetness. Mizu prioritises premium Japanese uni, selected at seasonal peak:

Bafun Uni
Deep orange, intensely rich and sweet — the connoisseur's favourite. Prized for its concentrated flavour.
Murasaki Uni
Pale yellow, cleaner and more delicate, with a refined sweetness and elegant finish.
Seasonal selection
The exact origin shifts with the season and the day's best trays — because peak uni can't be scheduled a year in advance.

Uni is graded and packed in wooden trays (ita) without additives when at its best. The finest grades are firm, bright and hold their shape — a sign of freshness Mizu insists on.

Different Preparations, One Ingredient

The heart of the Uni Tasting is seeing how one ingredient transforms across techniques:

Pure Nigiri

Uni draped over warm, lightly seasoned shari — the purest expression, nothing to hide behind.

Gunkan

Wrapped in crisp nori, where the seaweed's brine amplifies the urchin's sweetness.

Uni & Caviar

A luxurious pairing of sea urchin and cured roe — cream meets salt-pop.

Chilled & Composed

Served cool with complementary garnish to spotlight texture and finish.

Tasting them in sequence trains your palate: you start to notice temperature, the role of the rice, how nori changes everything, and which preparation you love most.
